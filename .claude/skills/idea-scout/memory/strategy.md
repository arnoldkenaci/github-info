## Learned rules

1. Don't propose cheap small items Temu already sells (under ~€30, light, generic). (seed)
2. Before calling a niche "missing", search at least 3 ways. An earlier run wrongly said premium baby brands were
   absent. (seed)
3. Prefer EU-, EFTA- or Turkish-made goods: Chinese-made goods pay full duty (often 10–15%) even via EU wholesalers. (seed)
4. Calibrate against the benchmark on the same scale. Host kits score ≈ 78 on this rubric (gap 6, duty 13, buyers 15,
   demand 15, capital 12, regulation 10, Temu-proof 7), against 54 from the advisors. An idea "beats host kits" only
   above ~78. Keep ≥ 50 as the shortlist bar, but rank and choose deep dives by the ~78 bar. (run 1)
5. With no Albania-specific demand evidence (demand points = 0), cap the score at 45 (status `new`), whatever the
   other criteria give. (run 1)
6. For clothing and textile ideas, search for local garment makers too ("prodhues uniforma", fason.al, PEA members):
   Albania has 875+ garment firms. Local sewing capacity counts as local competition. (run 1)
7. Merrjep pages come back empty to curl (JavaScript), so count Merrjep only through WebSearch (`site:merrjep.al ...`)
   and say "Merrjep unverified" when it was not reached. For products sold through offline agro or trade shops
   (beekeeping, farm tools), web searches miss sellers: phone-check before calling the gap "none". (run 1)
8. WebSearch rarely indexes small Albanian shops: results fill up with Zara Home, Airbnb, machineseeker.al and foreign
   SEO pages, and DuckDuckGo HTML returns a captcha to curl. For B2B-supply ideas, treat "no seller found" as
   "scarce (unverified)", never as "none", and write the phone check into `note`. (run 2)
9. Pages like apparelnbags.com/lp/<product>-<city>-albania are templated SEO landing pages, not local makers or
   shops. Don't count them as local competition or as local supply. (run 2)
10. If INSTAT shows the buyer population shrinking (e.g. cattle halved in a decade), give at most 5 demand points,
    however big the current count. (run 2)
11. INSTAT-producer niches pay off best (yield 2.5). When a producer niche is shortlisted, screen its repeat consumables
    (packaging, spare parts) the next run: they score higher on repeat buyers than the equipment itself. (run 2)
12. Before counting demand for a B2B supply, check whether a federation, ministry or donor already gives the product
    away or buys it centrally (e.g. FSHF gives every registered club free equipment; FAO tenders irrigation kit). If so,
    give at most 8 demand points and name the buyers left over. (run 3)
13. For agro and industrial inputs (film, crates, packaging), run one of the 3 competition searches as Albanian activity
    text ("prodhim dhe tregtim ... ", "injektim plastike arka") to surface opencorporates.al registry extracts: they found
    local makers that shop searches missed. A registry hit counts as "available", not "scarce". (run 3)
14. Make the Megateksa catalogue one of the 3 competition searches for every idea, and re-check open ideas with it.
    Search it with curl (see Sources) using English keywords. A Megateksa listing makes the gap "available", even for
    retail packs, and goes in `local_sellers` with the ALL price. In run 4 it found sellers that web searches had
    missed for 5 open ideas (corks, capsules, presses, oil bottles, jars, milkers, loppers). (run 4)
15. An idea whose gap is "scarce (unverified)" cannot be reported as beating host kits (~78), however high it scores. Write
    "beats on paper, gap unverified", and make the phone check the first step of its deep dive. Until run 4, every
    idea above ~78 rested on an unverified gap, and the former #1 (glass jars, 81) fell to 73 once checked. (run 4)
16. Bulky but very cheap goods (egg trays, peat, cardboard): estimate the value of one full pallet. If it is under
    ~€1,000, freight, the broker (~€80–150) and the €22 scanning fee eat most of the margin, so give at most 8 capital
    and complexity points and say so in `note`. Bulk alone makes an item Temu-proof, not profitable. (run 5)
17. If the biggest buyers in a niche can import full trucks themselves (large nurseries, exporters, chains), give at
    most 10 buyer points and name the smaller tier a beginner would serve. A beginner cannot undercut a buyer's own
    direct import. (run 5)
18. If a niche has fewer than ~50 identifiable buyers (e.g. ~25 Butrint mussel farms), give at most 8 buyer points and
    estimate the total yearly spend in `note`. A tiny market cannot carry a business, even with repeat orders; treat it as
    a side line to a bigger niche with the same supplier. (run 6)
19. For workshop and trade consumables (tyre shops, garages, builders, joiners), add a Würth Albania e-shop search
    (see Sources) to the 3 competition searches. Würth already sells these through sales reps, so a hit makes the gap
    "available". In run 6 it found wheel weights that Albanian web searches missed. (run 6)
20. If a 2025-26 rule change cuts the number of buyers (e.g. beach plots now rented only to hotels, ~60% fewer
    operators expected), treat it like a shrinking buyer base under rule 10: at most 5 demand points, and name the
    buyers left. (run 7)
21. For inputs that depend on a farming or building practice (silage baling, soilless growing, barrel-aged raki), first
    find evidence that the practice is common in Albania. Without it, give at most 3 demand points, however big the
    sector is. In run 7 two ideas (net wrap 73, coco slabs 64) rested on sector size alone. (run 7)
22. If a local manufacturer or converter makes the same finished product (not just a related one), e.g. cash-register
    rolls cut locally from imported jumbo paper, woven sacks, extrusions, call the gap "saturated" and cap the score at 49
    (status `new`). A beginner importing the finished product cannot undercut local production. Re-check open ideas
    that name a local maker (I-058 Albel, I-089 Pespa Alumin) against this rule first in run 9. (run 8)
23. Before calling a gap "scarce" for a raw or semi-processed product (charcoal, sacks, wood, stone), check OEC for Albanian
    exports of the same HS code and the registry/legal texts for local production (kilns, mills). Exports or a legal
    production route mean local makers exist: apply rule 22. In run 9 this capped restaurant charcoal at 49. (run 9)
24. Agro inputs that contain an active substance or an attractant lure (insecticide-baited traps, pheromone lures,
    biocides) count as plant-protection products: give at most 3 regulation points and check the MARD list of registered
    plant-protection products before any deep dive. Plain sticky cards and nets are not affected. (run 9)
