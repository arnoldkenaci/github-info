# Playbook: lessons from real success and failure stories

Lessons are advice to weigh, not guarantees. Each lesson cites the stories (IDs in `stories.jsonl`)
behind it. Confidence: high = 3+ independent stories incl. 1 verified; medium = 2+ or 1 verified; low = otherwise.
Most stories are founders' own accounts (self-reported); only 5 of 23 come from Albania or Kosovo, and none of
those is a failure. S-003, S-015 and S-016 are buyer feedback / buyer behaviour, not seller outcomes.

## What this means for the host-kit plan

1. **Do not count on hosts replacing linen "every season".** Hosts on forums replace sheets only when torn or stained,
   often after years; towels and face cloths wear out faster. Base the business on new listings and on high-turnover
   managed units, and offer towel refills as the repeat product. Re-check the plan's repeat-sales assumption. (L-005)
2. **Sell to property managers and cleaning companies first, not single-flat hosts.** Small hosts buy retail
   (Costco/Sam's Club-style, in Albania Megateksa); operators with many units are the ones who consider a bulk supply
   service. (L-017, L-006)
3. **Premium works face to face, not in anonymous listings.** Pitch reliability, a ready hotel set and time saved,
   with a sample in hand; a premium price on an online listing alone failed (NE Lounge). Sell a discounted
   sample/trial kit, so buyers can check quality before a bulk order. (L-006, L-020)
4. **Keep "no stock before commitments", and make commitments real.** Take deposits. A verbal yes or a
   demand spike is not demand; size the order on deposits. (L-001, L-013)
5. **Small first order, more checks, and a local fallback.** Samples and test orders still missed a bad batch
   (Lima Lima: 80% defective). Ask for photos/video of the real goods before shipping, and know which local
   supplier could cover an emergency. (L-002, L-003)
6. **Plan the off-season now.** Host demand is seasonal: land stock by late February, expect few sales from
   November to February, and add no fixed costs (staff, rented storage) until cash is banked. (L-008, L-018, L-019)
7. **Start through people you know, deliver the first orders yourself, then hand over to a courier**; keep every
   buyer's contact for re-orders and log the hours. (L-010, L-021, L-009, L-012)

## Lessons

### L-001: Get a real commitment (ideally a deposit) from the first buyers before building or buying stock
- Evidence: S-002, S-010, S-008 (negative), S-018 (negative; now uses pre-orders), S-021 (negative: research but no commitments) (n=5) · Counter-evidence: S-002 (first yes came during a spike that vanished) · Confidence: medium
- When it applies: any first order; B2B buyers you can meet in person.
- Note: no story yet shows a deposit being asked for and paid in a cash-on-delivery market (gap 7). Pre-orders also create
  an obligation: S-017's trade customers had pre-ordered when the stock arrived defective.

### L-002: Start with a very small range and a first order you can afford to lose
- Evidence: S-004 (3 towels), S-007 (50 products), S-008 (negative: printed too much), S-021 (negative: >$10,000 first run, $16,000 loss), S-022 (negative: $3,000 stock never fully sold), S-018 (negative: half the capital on one seasonal item) (n=6) · Counter-evidence: S-006 (widened fast, but mostly supplier-shipped) · Confidence: medium (all self-reported)
- When it applies: own-stock imports. Wide ranges only work when suppliers hold the stock.

### L-003: Sample several suppliers, write the spec down, check the real goods before they ship, and keep a local fallback
- Evidence: S-004 (8+ samples, quality "varies vastly"), S-011 (big order arrived as a different product), S-017 (samples and test orders passed, then 80% of 20,000 packs defective; rescued with local packaging), S-003 (factory matched a sample) (n=4) · Counter-evidence: – · Confidence: medium
- When it applies: textiles and any large order from a new supplier.

### L-004: Pack and sell in the small quantities small hosts can store and afford; skip fancy packaging
- Evidence: S-001 (cases of 4 or 8 kits), S-003 (hosts say no fancy packaging) (n=2) · Counter-evidence: S-010 (nice packaging got noticed in B2C) · Confidence: medium
- When it applies: B2B to small hosts. See L-017: small hosts are not the main buyers of a supply service.

### L-005: Hosts replace linen on wear, not on a schedule; towels wear faster than sheets, and only high-turnover units burn sheets fast
- Evidence: S-016 (sheets replaced only when torn/stained; 2015 linen still in use; ~7 sets culled 1 set in ~5 years; face cloths and towels replaced more), S-003 (one-night room rental used 4 sets/year; 4-night apartments kept 2015 bedding; "I go through towels faster than sheets") (n=2) · Counter-evidence: S-016 (one host replaces cheap sheets yearly) · Confidence: medium (forum anecdotes only)
- When it applies: forecasting repeat sales. Repeat demand comes from new listings, new units of managers, high-turnover units, cheap-linen users and towels.

### L-006: Win on quality and service where the buyer can see it; a low price is not a moat, and a premium price alone does not sell
- Evidence: S-002 (time saved), S-014 (reliable supply), S-003 (Costco anchor, "crowded field"), S-022 (low price as main advantage failed; premium custom work later succeeded) (n=4) · Counter-evidence: S-021 (premium product at $50 vs $20-40 did not sell on Amazon) · Confidence: medium
- When it applies: premium works with face-to-face selling, samples and service; it fails in anonymous marketplaces where buyers compare on price.

### L-007: On cash on delivery, confirm every order by phone/WhatsApp and take a deposit that covers shipping on large or unusual orders
- Evidence: S-005 (n=1) · Counter-evidence: – · Confidence: low
- When it applies: any COD sale, especially first orders from new areas. Matches the plan's 15% refusal rule.

### L-008: Land stock well before the season; delays, defects or stock-outs in the peak cost the year
- Evidence: S-012 (shipments stuck in customs at Christmas), S-004 (sold out, lost ranking), S-017 (35% of turnover in six weeks; late, defective shipment) (n=3) · Counter-evidence: – · Confidence: medium (all self-reported)

### L-009: Own the customer list (email/WhatsApp) instead of depending on one channel; repeat buyers come from it
- Evidence: S-001, S-004 (n=2) · Counter-evidence: – · Confidence: medium

### L-010: Win the first customers through relationships and audiences you already have
- Evidence: S-006 (B2B catalogue clients + own news portal), S-014 (family hotel's problem), S-002 (host network), S-019 (daughter set up Instagram; requests started immediately) (n=4, S-006 and S-019 verified) · Counter-evidence: – · Confidence: high

### L-011: For a part-timer, a few B2B accounts are easier than many B2C buyers
- Evidence: S-008, S-001, S-009, S-023 (club/team orders through one contact) (n=4) · Counter-evidence: S-023 (B2B still failed on seasonality and costs) · Confidence: medium (all self-reported)

### L-012: Count your hours, not just your money; a side business can fail by eating time
- Evidence: S-009 (support/refunds/billing not worth it next to a day job), S-008 (solo while working), S-019 (could not separate family time from the store) (n=3, S-019 verified) · Counter-evidence: – · Confidence: high

### L-013: Size stock on steady, paid demand, not on spikes from events, media or excitement
- Evidence: S-002 (convention rush vanished), S-013 (TV spike), S-018 (excitement over a seasonal item) (n=3, S-002 verified) · Counter-evidence: – · Confidence: high

### L-014: Expect a slow first year and thin margins; keep cash in reserve
- Evidence: S-014 (~8 months to results), S-004 (slim first-year margins), S-023 (year 1 revenue £6,500), S-022 (87 sales in 2.5 years) (n=4) · Counter-evidence: S-006 (100,000 euros in year 1, built on an existing business) · Confidence: medium

### L-015: In a small market, don't count on beating big players in search; use direct and niche channels
- Evidence: S-009, S-022 (self-taught SEO, penalty) (n=2) · Counter-evidence: S-008 (blog/SEO brought leads), S-009 (hosting reached page 1 in Slovenian), S-023 (ranked top for a niche) · Confidence: low (conflicting)
- When it applies: SEO works for a narrow niche with little local competition; not against established chains.

### L-017: Bulk supply programmes appeal to operators with many units; small hosts buy retail
- Evidence: S-015 (PM with 65+ units interested; small investor uses Sam's Club drop-ship), S-003 (hosts anchor on Costco), S-001 (a 120-unit host restocked all units at once) (n=3) · Counter-evidence: S-001 (starter hosts also buy small cases online) · Confidence: medium
- When it applies: choosing the first 30 prospects: property managers, cleaning companies, guesthouses.

### L-018: A seasonal business needs an off-season plan (cash reserve or second product line)
- Evidence: S-023 (summer-only sports kit: £40k month then £550; liquidated), S-018 (seasonal stock unsold by January), S-017 (35% of turnover in six weeks) (n=3) · Counter-evidence: – · Confidence: medium
- When it applies: host demand peaks before and during the tourist season.

### L-019: Do not add fixed costs (staff, premises) before cash is in the bank; fast growth can kill through overtrading
- Evidence: S-023 (salesperson quadrupled processing cost; overtrading), S-013 (growth pressure before costs were controlled) (n=2) · Counter-evidence: S-017 (full-time + premises worked, after 1.5 years of proven sales) · Confidence: medium

### L-020: Sell a cheap sample or trial pack to B2B buyers before the bespoke or bulk order
- Evidence: S-023 (discounted sizing-sample packs for clubs), S-004 (samples decided supplier choice; buyer side) (n=2) · Counter-evidence: – · Confidence: medium (self-reported)
- When it applies: the plan's 2 sample kits; consider charging a small, refundable fee.

### L-021: Deliver the first orders yourself; switch to a courier when distance or volume grows
- Evidence: S-019 (delivered by car, then express post), S-007 (staff delivered, built word of mouth) (n=2, S-019 verified) · Counter-evidence: – · Confidence: medium

## Other lessons (not directly relevant to the current plan)

### L-016: Rental/subscription models need cheap stock and tight logistics before growth
- Evidence: S-013, S-002 (n=2) · Confidence: medium. Relevant only if the plan moves toward linen rental.

### L-022: In Kosovo/Albania, Instagram/Facebook is a low-cost start for B2C; add a simple website with categories as the range grows
- Evidence: S-019, S-020 (n=2, verified) · Confidence: medium. Relevant to the Petkit backup or a later consumer line, not to B2B host kits.

### L-023: Don't pick a first product you have never used, in a niche you don't know, on a platform you don't understand
- Evidence: S-021, S-022 (n=2) · Confidence: medium.
