# Playbook: lessons from real success and failure stories

Lessons are advice to weigh, not guarantees. Each lesson cites the stories (IDs in `stories.jsonl`)
behind it. Confidence: high = 3+ independent stories incl. 1 verified; medium = 2+ or 1 verified; low = otherwise.
Most stories are founders' own accounts (self-reported); 50 of 93 come from Albania or Kosovo. The first Albanian
failures (S-036, S-037, S-038, run 5) are three small Tirana clothing shops from one 2020 Monitor survey, all hit by the
earthquake and COVID-19: treat them as one source when judging confidence. S-043, S-044 and S-045 (run 6) are three
small Albanian shops from a second Monitor survey (February 2018, no shock: falling turnover, rent, taxes, a loan);
they count as one more source. S-046 is the first Albanian B2B seller to hotels and Airbnb apartments (an incumbent
hotel-textile supplier, self-reported interview). S-051, S-052 and S-053 (run 7) are three Albanian online sellers quoted in
one 2018 Monitor feature on online retail (one source); S-049 is a 2006 USAID story on an Albanian towel maker. Other Balkan stories: Serbia (S-024 failure,
S-030, S-031, S-032, S-035), North Macedonia (S-034, a hotel-textile maker) and Montenegro (S-033). S-003, S-015, S-016,
S-027, S-028, S-040 and S-042 are buyer feedback / buyer behaviour, and S-041 and S-050 (a Romanian advertorial) are
companies' own offers, not seller outcomes. Run 8 added Tirana hosts as buyer evidence (S-057 and S-058 from one 2025
Monitor article, both left daily rentals; S-059 from a 2022 article), a Tirana fit-out firm's claim (S-060), an Albanian
B2B cleaning firm (S-056) and two Kosovo shops squeezed by supermarkets (S-054 failure, S-055; one article). Run 9
added two Kosovo Instagram thrift sellers (S-061, S-062; one 2021 article), a Tirana flower importer and an Elbasan
grower hit by COVID (S-063, S-064; one 2021 Monitor feature), two Serbian online failures (S-065 shop, S-066
marketplace; Forbes Srbija) and a Serbian shop holding on next to chains (S-067). Run 10 added three Tirana used-clothing
sellers (S-070 failure, S-071, S-072; one 2019 Monitor article, first names only), a Durrës carpentry selling to hotels
(S-073), two Albanian sanitary-ware importers on short-term-rental demand (S-074, S-075; one 2026 Monitor article, market

(S-068) and a Serbian small-town bookshop (S-069). Run 11 added a Tirana boutique importing Turkish fashion since 1995 that

## What this means for the host-kit plan

1. **Do not count on hosts replacing linen "every season".** Hosts on forums replace sheets only when torn or stained,
   often after years; towels and face cloths wear out faster. An Albanian hotel-textile supplier says hotels change
   linen once every 2-3 years, sooner where poor laundries ruin it. Base the business on new listings and on
   high-turnover managed units, and offer towel refills as the repeat product. Re-check the plan's repeat-sales
   assumption (it assumes every season). One Tirana host with three busy flats did say sheets and towels "had to be
   bought often", but she has since left daily rentals, so frequent re-buying is a cost that pushes small hosts out,
   not a loyal repeat market. New listings are slowing too: Monitor reports only +1.1k units nationally in H1 2026
   (27.7k listed, +4.2% on the 2025 average), so do not size the plan on 2025's new-listing counts. (L-005, L-035)
2. **Sell to property managers and cleaning companies first, not single-flat hosts.** Small hosts buy retail
   (Costco/Sam's Club-style, in Albania Megateksa); operators with many units are the ones who consider a bulk supply
   service. French managers increasingly own professional linen and bundle it into an all-inclusive fee (25% with
   linen vs 20% without; most owners took the 25%), so pitch Tirana managers a linen package they can resell to owners
   inside their fee, and ask whether they or the flat owner own the linen today: the owner of the linen is your buyer.
   An Athens manager does the same (premium service with hotel linen, or linen sold to owners at preferential prices).
   Offer no long contract: French managers paid 15,000 euros to leave a 4-year linen contract, and Athens linen-delivery
   services now pitch "no contracts, no upfront investment"; sell kits the buyer owns, with refills on demand.
   **New in run 8:** Tirana's small hosts are leaving daily rentals (nightly prices fell from 60 to 30-40 euros in
   2022-2024; two hosts quoted by Monitor quit), while rentals concentrate in professional operators. Put the big
   operators first on the call list (PikHost, about 136 listings, co-founder of the Albanian Vacation Rental
   Association, which is also a route to many operators at once). Reach new hosts at fit-out through furnishers and
   interior architects who set flats up, since a new host buys everything once. (L-035, L-036)
   **New in run 10:** Albanian sanitary-ware importers now name short-term-rental investors as a main source of demand
   in place of families and emigrants, and say these buyers want products that stand up to intensive use (Monitor,
   Aug 2026), which backs selling at fit-out with a durability pitch. AirDNA counted almost 27,000 units at end-March
   2026: 5.3k in Tirana, almost 4k in Durrës, but 10.3k in Vlorë and Sarandë, so keep Vlorë/Sarandë operators as the
   second call list once Tirana/Durrës is tested (our inference).
   **New in run 14:** Durrës looks like Tirana. In the beach district most daily-rental flats are leased by people who
   manage them as a business, some owners hire cleaning and management teams, stays are 1-5 days with a full clean after
   each, and nightly prices have fallen to about 25 euros (BIRN, 2025-2026). A traditional room renter's income halved in
   2025. So in Durrës call the leaseholder-managers and the cleaning teams owners hire, not individual owners, and pitch
   cost per turnover (towel refills, a spare set per flat) rather than premium textiles. Durrës hotels lost most Kosovar
   and Italian guests in 2026 and now fill with 7-day groups from Poland, Czechia, Bosnia and Ukraine. (L-035, L-017, L-005)
   (L-017, L-025, L-028, L-006)
3. **Premium works face to face, not in anonymous listings, and never try to win on price.** Pitch reliability,
   a ready hotel set and time saved, with a sample in hand; a premium price on an online listing alone failed
   (NE Lounge), and a Serbian shop lost to buyers who compared it with 99-dinar internet offers. Leave lockboxes and
   other small items that buyers can price on Temu inside the kit only. Sell a discounted sample/trial kit, so buyers
   can check quality before a bulk order. Sell direct, not through shops or chains: a Macedonian hotel-textile maker
   dropped Vero, Mercator and Metro because they resold at 3 euros what they bought at 1.8. Managers already have a
   supplier, so bring a concrete reason to switch (a free trial set, a delivery date you can keep). The rival is not
   only Megateksa: Gjergji H. Tekstil (in the market since 1994, 25 tailors making custom sizes) already sells quality
   towels and sheets to Airbnb apartments in Tirana, Durrës and Vlorë, and in 2024 planned a laundry and daily linen
   rental. Get its prices and terms before setting yours, and win on a ready kit per unit delivered to the door, refills
   on demand and no contract, not on "hotel quality" alone. Expect unregistered sellers to undercut any price you set
   (a Durrës hotel-furniture maker and an Albanian online portal both lose on price to informal rivals); do not chase
   their price, and sell to registered operators and hotels who need a fiscal invoice, a guarantee and a supplier that
   will still be there next season. A registered Albanian clothing shop pays about 40,000 lek a month in rent and services
   plus insurance, municipal tax and an accountant before it sells anything, while a 20-year-old Instagram seller said
   registering would force her to close: informal prices are low because informal sellers skip those costs (Faktoje, 2019).
   **New in run 11:** do not price like a Tirana boutique that marks Turkish goods up
   about three times: a Bardhyl Street shop selling Turkish fashion since 1995 lost 30-40% of demand in a year as malls
   took half the high-street market, and a retail analyst says Albanian buyers now compare such prices online and order
   from abroad. Managers can look up Turkish towel prices too, so show the landed cost plus a clear service fee (kit
   packing, delivery, refills), not a retail markup (our inference). **New in run 13:** add JYSK to the price check: it has six
   Albanian stores (company-owned from 1 Jan 2027), duvets and pillows are its third-best category, and its manager said the
   dearest 'gold' tier sells best (2018). A host can buy a premium set at a mall, so the kit must win on a ready set per
   unit delivered, refills and an invoice, not on the textile alone. An Albanian furniture maker that sold through traders
   lost touch with buyers and was undercut, then opened its own shops: keep selling direct (FPM, 2019). (L-006, L-020, L-027, L-030, L-031, L-028, L-040, L-042)
4. **Keep "no stock before commitments", and make commitments real.** Take deposits. A verbal yes or a
   demand spike is not demand; size the order on deposits. Get paid at delivery or before (deposit, then balance on
   handover); never let a manager run an open tab, since unpaid service fees are common in this trade. Expect Albanian
   buyers to resist paying up front: over 90% of online sales are cash on delivery because informal sellers have taken
   money and not delivered. Earn the deposit with a registered company, a fiscal invoice, a sample in hand and a
   small amount (our inference: 20-30%), not by asking strangers to prepay online. **New in run 9:** likes and views
   are not commitments either: a Belgrade online shop's posts reached tens of thousands and at first nobody bought,
   and the same shop closed after it crossed the VAT threshold without an accountant and lost the grant that paid its
   staff. Price every kit with 20% VAT from the first import (an importer is very likely VAT-registered anyway),
   contract an accountant before the first sale, keep cash aside for stock that is tied up, and pay no running cost
   from a grant. **New in run 12:** a tourism forecast is not an order either: an Albanian brewer added 15,000 hl for an
   expected +10% tourist summer in 2025, consumption fell 1-2% and the stock sat in its warehouse. Do not order extra
   kits for "a record season"; order on managers' deposits only. **New in run 13:** Albanian online buyers' top complaints are wrong goods (70% in a
   Citizens.al survey), refused returns and no fiscal receipt, so put the kit spec in writing, hand a fiscal receipt with every
   delivery and give a written exchange policy; that is what makes a deposit believable (our inference). **New in run 14:**
   in 2022, 80% of Albanian online buyers surveyed bought through Facebook/Instagram and 75% had problems; pages blocked
   buyers who complained, and 18 of 20+ pages had no website, phone or address. Put the company name, NIPT, address and a
   phone number on every quote and invoice. Budget the accountant as a fixed cost: one small registered shop paid 365,000
   old lek (about 36,500 lek) a year in 2019 (one data point; re-check current fees). (L-001, L-013, L-029, L-034, L-037, L-038, L-014)
5. **Small first order, more checks, a local fallback, and no promised dates before goods are in hand.** Samples
   and test orders still missed a bad batch (Lima Lima: 80% defective). One beginner that grew bought 100 units,
   sold them, then bought 200 (CROSSNET); its pre-orders slipped from weeks to months when stock was late. Write the spec (gsm, size, colour, fibre) in
   numbers in the supplier's working language, check it yourself, and ask for photos/video of the real goods before
   shipping. Ask the supplier about holiday shutdowns and lead time before quoting a delivery date to a manager
   (a missed date lost Vineyard Sun a repeat client); know which local supplier could cover an emergency: Albania had its own towel maker (Florjan-V, a quarter of the
   domestic towel market in 2006, S-049); check whether it or another local maker still produces before relying on
   Turkey alone. Buy from the maker, not from an Albanian wholesaler: an Albanian online seller found local suppliers
   costly and cut prices by contracting foreign suppliers directly (S-052), and a Kosovo wholesaler charged a small shop
   88 cents for an item supermarkets got at 75 cents (S-054). **New in run 10:** do not haggle the first order with a
   new Turkish maker: a Serbian curtain importer found that each price cut on a first order came back as lower quality,
   so he negotiates only from the second or third order. Pay the asking price for the 2 sample kits and the first small
   order, write gsm and size into the order, and ask for a discount on the re-order. (L-002, L-003, L-024, L-033, L-039)
6. **Plan the off-season now.** Host demand is seasonal: Albanian hotel-textile orders for summer start in January
   and peak from April to June (S-046), so pitch managers in December-January, land stock by late February, and expect
   few sales from August to December, and add no fixed costs (staff, rented storage, a shop) until cash is banked: three Tirana shops
   with rent to pay closed when sales fell in 2020, and a Tirana boutique kept trading only after it dropped the shop and
   sold through Facebook/Instagram (S-051). A Serbian HoReCa supplier covers its off-season by selling to mountain
   and spa destinations; the Albanian equivalent (our inference) is Tirana city flats that rent all year and mountain
   guesthouses, plus branded towels for companies as a winter line (S-034). Do not fund stock or premises with a loan:
   an Elbasan shop owner still repays hers after closing (S-044). (L-008, L-018, L-019)
7. **Start through people you know, deliver the first orders yourself, then hand over to a courier**; keep every
   buyer's contact for re-orders and ask happy buyers for referrals (a Serbian online shop grew mainly on returning
   buyers and referrals); log the hours: two Serbian founders who ran shops next to a job or a baby closed when the
   weekly operations outgrew them. (L-010, L-021, L-009, L-012)

## Lessons

### L-001: Get a real commitment (ideally a deposit) from the first buyers before building or buying stock
- Evidence: S-002, S-010, S-008 (negative), S-018 (negative; now uses pre-orders), S-021 (negative: research but no commitments), S-065 (negative: Belgrade online food shop, posts seen by tens of thousands but at first nobody bought; reach is not demand) (n=6) · Counter-evidence: S-002 (first yes came during a spike that vanished) · Confidence: medium
- When it applies: any first order; B2B buyers you can meet in person.
- Note: no story yet shows a deposit being asked for and paid in a cash-on-delivery market (gap 5). Conditional
  counter-evidence S-048: CROSSNET took orders before stock was in and deliveries slipped from weeks to months; pre-sell
  only with a delivery date you can keep (L-024). Conditional counter-evidence S-050 (Romanian advertorial, an offer):
  an established hotel-textile importer sells on 150+ pallets in stock and 24-48-hour delivery; holding stock works
  once demand is proven, not for a first order. Pre-orders also create
  an obligation: S-017's trade customers had pre-ordered when the stock arrived defective.

### L-002: Start with a very small range and a first order you can afford to lose
- Evidence: S-004 (3 towels), S-007 (50 products), S-008 (negative: printed too much), S-021 (negative: >$10,000 first run, $16,000 loss), S-022 (negative: $3,000 stock never fully sold), S-018 (negative: half the capital on one seasonal item), S-024 (negative: 30-50k euros of stock, only ~5% turned; expired paints a pure loss), S-048 (first order 100 units, sold, then 200; later 7,500), S-079 (Albanian cosmetics importer started in 1994 with 2 Italian hair-dye brands and grew to 8 lines over ~25 years) (n=9) · Counter-evidence: S-006 (widened fast, but mostly supplier-shipped) · Confidence: medium (all self-reported)
- When it applies: own-stock imports. Wide ranges only work when suppliers hold the stock.

### L-003: Sample several suppliers, write the spec down, check the real goods before they ship, and keep a local fallback
- Evidence: S-004 (8+ samples, quality "varies vastly"), S-011 (big order arrived as a different product), S-017 (samples and test orders passed, then 80% of 20,000 packs defective; rescued with local packaging), S-003 (factory matched a sample), S-026 (anecdote: mistranslated spec, wrong colours/sizes/materials), S-031 (Serbian B2B maker keeps alternative sources in China, Italy, Pakistan and Turkey so supply never stops), S-046 (Albanian hotel-textile supplier: international hotel brands specify every item in cm and grams; advises hotels to set a written quality standard), S-049 (a local Albanian towel maker existed in 2006, a possible local fallback; status today unknown), S-066 (negative: a Serbian marketplace closed after its small suppliers shipped late, wrong or broken goods and refused refunds; the buyer blamed the platform), S-068 (Serbian curtain importer: a new Turkish supplier lowered quality whenever he pushed the price down) (n=10) · Counter-evidence: – · Confidence: medium (all self-reported or anecdote)
- When it applies: textiles and any large order from a new supplier. Check the written spec yourself even if an agent or translator handles the order (S-026).

### L-004: Pack and sell in the small quantities small hosts can store and afford; skip fancy packaging
- Evidence: S-001 (cases of 4 or 8 kits), S-003 (hosts say no fancy packaging) (n=2) · Counter-evidence: S-010 (nice packaging got noticed in B2C) · Confidence: medium
- When it applies: B2B to small hosts. See L-017: small hosts are not the main buyers of a supply service.

### L-005: Hosts replace linen on wear, not on a schedule; towels wear faster than sheets, and only high-turnover units burn sheets fast
- Evidence: S-016 (sheets replaced only when torn/stained; 2015 linen still in use; ~7 sets culled 1 set in ~5 years; face cloths and towels replaced more), S-003 (one-night room rental used 4 sets/year; 4-night apartments kept 2015 bedding; "I go through towels faster than sheets"), S-046 (Albanian supplier: hotels change linen once every 2-3 years, partly because poor laundries' detergents ruin it; a seller's claim) (n=3) · Counter-evidence: S-016 (one host replaces cheap sheets yearly), S-057 (conditional: a Tirana host with three daily-rental flats said "sheets and towels had to be bought often" in 2022-2024; anecdote) · Supports the high-turnover clause: S-090 (Durrës B&B guests stay 1-5 days, full clean after each, 2025) · Confidence: medium (forum anecdotes only)
- When it applies: forecasting repeat sales. Repeat demand comes from new listings, new units of managers, high-turnover units, cheap-linen users and towels.
- Note (run 8): the only Albanian host quoted on linen (S-057) found frequent re-buying a burden of busy Tirana flats, which fits high-turnover units burning linen fast; it does not show a schedule, and that host has since left daily rentals.

### L-006: Win on quality and service where the buyer can see it; a low price is not a moat, and a premium price alone does not sell
- Evidence: S-002 (time saved), S-014 (reliable supply), S-003 (Costco anchor, "crowded field"), S-022 (low price as main advantage failed; premium custom work later succeeded), S-024 (Serbian shop could not match 99-dinar internet offers; buyers saw its 6,000-dinar set as overpriced), S-030 (Serbian online appliance shop stands out by courtesy and service; competition is its biggest challenge), S-033 (Montenegrin B2B distributor: be a serious, reliable partner), S-034 (pure-cotton hotel textiles sell to coastal hotels, not to Serbian buyers who switched to cheap polyester), S-039 (negative: 2-3x the market price with "not a much better product" failed), S-046 (Albanian supplier: Airbnb apartments in Tirana/Durrës/Vlorë ask for quality towels and sheets and "very rarely" for cheap ones; a seller's claim), S-052 (Albanian online seller: saturated market, most operators sell near cost, some leave every year), S-050 (Romanian hotel-textile importer sells on stock and 24-48 h delivery; advertorial), S-054 (negative: a Kosovo shop selling the same goods as supermarkets at higher prices closed within a year), S-055 (negative: same article, income halved), S-069 (Serbian small-town bookshop has lasted 20 years next to big markets by insisting on quality and good stock, 'sometimes to my own cost'), S-071 (negative: a Tirana used-clothing seller's buyers ask for discounts she cannot give), S-074 (Albanian sanitary-ware importer: short-term-rental buyers want products that resist intensive use; importer's claim), S-077 (negative: a Tirana boutique selling Turkish imports since 1995 lost 30-40% of demand in a year to malls; an analyst in the same article says a 3x markup on Turkish clothes makes buyers hesitate because they compare online; anecdote), S-078 (negative: a Myslym Shyri clothing shop loses buyers who look for cheap prices to malls and used-clothing markets; anecdote), S-084 (JYSK Albania, 2018: of its basic, plus and gold home-furnishing tiers the dearest 'gold' sells best, and it sells on guarantee and durability the neighbourhood market lacks; large chain, self-reported) (n=20) · Counter-evidence: S-072 (conditional: a Tirana used-clothing seller makes 45-50k lekë a month selling cheap to poor buyers in a low-income area), S-021 (premium product at $50 vs $20-40 did not sell on Amazon), S-067 (conditional: a Kraljevo household-chemicals shop has held on for 16 years next to dm and Lilly by being 'somewhat cheaper' plus professional service and larger orders for institutions; results slipped in 2025) · Confidence: medium
- When it applies: premium works with face-to-face selling, samples and service; it fails in anonymous marketplaces where buyers compare on price, and on small items that buyers can price on Chinese platforms (S-024). Quality sells to buyers whose guests notice it (coastal and spa hotels, S-034); price-driven buyers move to cheap polyester (S-034, Serbia).

### L-007: On cash on delivery, confirm every order by phone/WhatsApp and take a deposit that covers shipping on large or unusual orders
- Evidence: S-005, S-052 (Albanian market players: about 30% of online clothing orders are returned), S-086 (Morocco, Oct 2026: non-food COD returns can reach 30-40%; some sellers keep teams that confirm orders by phone before shipping; cash takes days to reach the seller; market comment by Glovo Maroc's director) (n=3) · Counter-evidence: – · Confidence: low (self-reported; S-052 is about clothing B2C and S-086 is market comment, not a seller's own numbers)
- When it applies: any COD sale, especially first orders from new areas. Matches the plan's 15% refusal rule; two COD markets (Albania clothing ~30%, Morocco non-food 30-40%) report returns well above 15% for anonymous B2C, which is one more reason to sell kits B2B face to face (L-011).

### L-008: Land stock well before the season; delays, defects or stock-outs in the peak cost the year
- Evidence: S-012 (shipments stuck in customs at Christmas), S-004 (sold out, lost ranking), S-017 (35% of turnover in six weeks; late, defective shipment), S-025 (supplier holiday shutdown delayed a re-order), S-046 (Albanian hotel-textile supplier: 2024 summer orders started in January; April-June volume means overtime) (n=5) · Counter-evidence: – · Confidence: medium (all self-reported)

### L-009: Own the customer list (email/WhatsApp) instead of depending on one channel; repeat buyers come from it
- Evidence: S-001, S-004, S-030 (returning buyers and referrals drive growth next to a marketplace listing), S-077 (a 30-year Tirana boutique's remaining demand comes mainly from loyal customers after malls took half the market; anecdote) (n=4) · Counter-evidence: – · Confidence: medium (all self-reported)

### L-010: Win the first customers through relationships and audiences you already have
- Evidence: S-006 (B2B catalogue clients + own news portal), S-014 (family hotel's problem), S-002 (host network), S-019 (daughter set up Instagram; requests started immediately), S-029 (a laundry business grew out of a conciergerie client), S-047 (friends sold MP3 players with flyers; word of mouth and referrals, no paid marketing), S-062 (Kosovo sisters started an Instagram thrift shop with clothes they already had, then bought stock once sales rose) (n=7, S-006 and S-019 verified) · Counter-evidence: – · Confidence: high

### L-011: For a part-timer, a few B2B accounts are easier than many B2C buyers
- Evidence: S-008, S-001, S-009, S-023 (club/team orders through one contact), S-079 (Albanian cosmetics importer supplies large hairdressing and beauty centres and reads retail demand through them) (n=5) · Counter-evidence: S-023 (B2B still failed on seasonality and costs) · Confidence: medium (all self-reported)

### L-012: Count your hours, not just your money; a side business can fail by eating time
- Evidence: S-009 (support/refunds/billing not worth it next to a day job), S-008 (solo while working), S-019 (could not separate family time from the store), S-065 (wanted a part-time shop next to a baby; it needed full weekly operations and she closed it), S-066 (three founders with day jobs ran a marketplace for four years, earned nothing and ran out of enthusiasm) (n=5, S-019 verified) · Counter-evidence: – · Confidence: high

### L-013: Size stock on steady, paid demand, not on spikes from events, media or excitement
- Evidence: S-002 (convention rush vanished), S-013 (TV spike), S-018 (excitement over a seasonal item) (n=3, S-002 verified), S-082 (Albanian brewer added 15,000 hl for an expected +10% tourist summer; consumption fell 1-2% and stock piled up in 2025; larger firm, relevance 2), S-093 (Durrës hotels lost most Kosovar and Italian guests in 2026 after years of loyalty; the guest mix shifts from one summer to the next) (n=5) · Counter-evidence: – · Confidence: high
- Note (run 12): S-082 is the first Albanian case of a tourism forecast, not a buyer order, driving stock; 2025's tourists were 'low cost' and stayed in Booking/Airbnb apartments.

### L-014: Expect a slow first year and thin margins; keep cash in reserve
- Evidence: S-014 (~8 months to results), S-004 (slim first-year margins), S-023 (year 1 revenue £6,500), S-022 (87 sales in 2.5 years), S-030 (ranking of Serbian micro online retailers: e.g. 112m RSD revenue for a 60k RSD result), S-032 ("sometimes you work without profit"), S-039 (no break-even after ~18 months; $800 revenue vs $1,200 monthly burn), S-065 (did not budget for cash tied up in stock, chased supplier invoices weekly; a cash reserve let her close cleanly), S-069 ('the first three years are the hardest: small earnings, big effort') (n=9) · Counter-evidence: S-006 (100,000 euros in year 1, built on an existing business) · Confidence: medium

### L-015: In a small market, don't count on beating big players in search; use direct and niche channels
- Evidence: S-009, S-022 (self-taught SEO, penalty) (n=2) · Counter-evidence: S-008 (blog/SEO brought leads), S-009 (hosting reached page 1 in Slovenian), S-023 (ranked top for a niche) · Confidence: low (conflicting)
- When it applies: SEO works for a narrow niche with little local competition; not against established chains.

### L-017: Bulk supply programmes appeal to operators with many units; small hosts buy retail
- Evidence: S-015 (PM with 65+ units interested; small investor uses Sam's Club drop-ship), S-003 (hosts anchor on Costco), S-001 (a 120-unit host restocked all units at once), S-027 (French conciergerie buys hotel-quality linen itself and rents it to owners; anecdote), S-028 (a 30-unit French conciergerie includes linen in its single 25% offer), S-040 (Athens manager offers hotel linen in premium services or sells linen to owners), S-041 (Athens linen-delivery service sells to "owners and managers"), S-057 and S-058 (Tirana: single-flat and small hosts left daily rentals in 2024 as prices fell; Monitor reports daily rentals concentrating in professional operators, one source), S-090 (Durrës: a leaseholder runs two flats as a business; some owners hire cleaning and management teams), S-092 (Durrës: most daily-rental flats leased by people who manage them) (n=11) · Counter-evidence: S-001 (starter hosts also buy small cases online) · Confidence: medium
- When it applies: choosing the first 30 prospects: property managers, cleaning companies, guesthouses. Ask who owns the linen (manager or flat owner); that party is the buyer (S-027). In Tirana the small-host segment is shrinking (L-035), so operators matter even more.

### L-018: A seasonal business needs an off-season plan (cash reserve or second product line)
- Evidence: S-023 (summer-only sports kit: £40k month then £550; liquidated), S-018 (seasonal stock unsold by January), S-017 (35% of turnover in six weeks), S-035 (Serbian HoReCa supplier: summer peak, rest of year covered by mountain and spa destinations), S-064 (Albanian grower: one-season plants for hotels and flower shops were destroyed unsold when buyers closed in 2020; COVID shock), S-063 (Tirana flower importer depends on the summer wedding peak; same 2021 Monitor article), S-073 (Durrës hotel-furniture maker: spring-summer peak met with seasonal workers) (n=7; S-063/S-064 one source, shock-driven) · Counter-evidence: – · Confidence: medium
- When it applies: host demand peaks before and during the tourist season. One off-season answer seen: sell to destinations with a different season (S-035), or a second B2B line such as branded towels for companies (S-034).

### L-019: Do not add fixed costs (staff, premises) before cash is in the bank; fast growth can kill through overtrading
- Evidence: S-023 (salesperson quadrupled processing cost; overtrading), S-013 (growth pressure before costs were controlled), S-032 (33-year Serbian shop: "don't chase money, don't fall into debt"; 14 m2 to 400 m2 slowly), S-036 (Tirana shop: rent of 90,000 lek a month plus taxes, bankrupt after one year), S-038 (Tirana shop of 14 years closed rather than take on debt), S-043 (15-year Albanian curtain/clothing shop could not cover expenses and taxes when turnover fell, 2018), S-044 (Elbasan cosmetics shop opened on a bank loan: obligations from month one, loan still repaid after closing), S-045 (Tirana shop: rent too high, transport from Korça not covered), S-051 (Tirana boutique closed its shop to stop paying high rent and sold online only), S-088 (southern Albanian clothing shop: about 40,000 lek a month in rent and services plus yearly insurance, municipal tax, accountant and till inspection, 'every day starts at minus 17,000 old lek'; anecdote, 2019) (n=10; S-036-S-038 are one 2020 COVID survey and S-043-S-045 one 2018 survey with no shock) · Counter-evidence: S-017 (full-time + premises worked, after 1.5 years of proven sales), S-049 (Albanian towel maker grew with loans for equipment, a generator and a delivery van; productive assets, not stock or rent) · Confidence: medium

### L-020: Sell a cheap sample or trial pack to B2B buyers before the bespoke or bulk order
- Evidence: S-023 (discounted sizing-sample packs for clubs), S-004 (samples decided supplier choice; buyer side) (n=2) · Counter-evidence: – · Confidence: medium (self-reported)
- When it applies: the plan's 2 sample kits; consider charging a small, refundable fee.

### L-021: Deliver the first orders yourself; switch to a courier when distance or volume grows
- Evidence: S-019 (delivered by car, then express post), S-007 (staff delivered, built word of mouth), S-049 (Albanian towel maker bought its own minivan to deliver to retailers and wholesalers) (n=3, S-019 verified) · Counter-evidence: – · Confidence: high

### L-024: Do not promise a B2B delivery date until you know the supplier's lead time and holiday shutdowns, or until the goods are in hand
- Evidence: S-025 (re-order delayed by a ~1-month Chinese New Year shutdown; client lost), S-017 (trade customers had pre-ordered when a late, defective shipment arrived), S-012 (shipments stuck in customs at Christmas), S-048 (orders taken before stock was in: "a few weeks turned into a few months"; now says have stock in or set expectations for the worst) (n=4, all self-reported) · Counter-evidence: – · Confidence: medium
- When it applies: every manager order taken on deposit. For Turkish suppliers, ask about public-holiday shutdowns and the factory's real lead time in writing before quoting a date (this application to Turkey is our inference, not from a story; gap 3).

### L-025: Offer property managers linen they can bundle into an all-inclusive fee, not just sheets at a unit price
- Evidence: S-028 (OC Keys: one 25% offer with linen included), S-027 (the same podcast's host: owners offered 20% with their own linen or 25% all-inclusive with professional linen; most chose 25%, per the episode "De 20 à 25 % de commission"; anecdote), S-040 (Athens manager Cloudkeys: professional hotel linen in its premium services, or linen supplied to owners at preferential prices) (n=3, two countries, all self-reported) · Counter-evidence: – · Confidence: medium
- When it applies: pitching managers who charge owners a commission. Sell them a package (sets per unit, refills, maybe laundry) that lets them raise their fee or resell linen to owners. Seen in France and Greece; not yet in Albania (gap 12).

### L-026: Grow supply businesses from inside a client base you already serve
- Evidence: S-029 (a conciergerie added a laundry born from one of its clients and bought a detergent distributor), S-014 (hotel linen supplier born from the family hotel's problem), S-035 (Serbian egg producer added imported ingredients to sell through its existing customer network), S-056 (Albanian cleaning firm AlbPastrim sells cleaning products and staff training to the hotels and residences it serves) (n=4, self-reported; S-056 turnover verified) · Counter-evidence: – · Confidence: medium
- When it applies: once the first managers buy kits, the next products (refills, laundry, cleaning consumables) should come from their requests, not from a catalogue.

### L-027: Sell direct to hotels and managers; do not hand the margin to shops or chains
- Evidence: S-034 (Macedonian hotel-textile maker left Vero, Mercator and Metro: bought at 1.8 euros, resold at 3; direct buyers do best because they skip middlemen's costs), S-085 (Albanian furniture maker FPM opened its own shops in 2017-18 to sell direct and cut out traders, after losing contact with Albanian customers and seeing the shops it supplied undercut; larger firm) (n=2, self-reported) · Counter-evidence: – · Confidence: medium
- When it applies: a premium kit whose value needs explaining. A shop shelf puts it next to Megateksa prices with no one to explain it. Confirm with a second story (gap 17).

### L-028: Do not lock B2B buyers into long contracts; sell an asset they own, with optional refills
- Evidence: S-042 (French managers: a 4-year linen contract cost 15,000 euros to exit; warnings about "miracle solutions"; anecdote), S-041 (Athens linen-delivery competitor sells on "no contracts" and "no upfront investment in linens"), S-046 (the Albanian incumbent planned a 12-tonne-per-shift laundry and daily linen rental for small and medium hotels in 2024: linen-as-a-service may reach Tirana) S-056 (Albania: many potential clients see no advantage in outsourcing, and hotels and residence complexes often want their own staff; AlbPastrim sold them training and products instead) (n=4, anecdote, company page, interviews) · Counter-evidence: S-028 (managers do sign up for bundled linen when it raises their fee) · Confidence: medium (no story yet of a Balkan buyer choosing owned kits over rental)
- When it applies: pitching managers. Linen-as-a-service may reach Tirana (gap 16); answer it with owned kits, no lock-in and a lower cost per stay over a season.

### L-029: Get paid at handover (or before) and agree the payment method when the deal is signed
- Evidence: S-042 (8,000 euros unpaid on one villa, recovered only by threatening to block the listing, client lost anyway; a direct-debit mandate signed with the contract is the protection "very few" use; anecdote), S-005 (COD buyers refuse parcels without a deposit) (n=2, anecdote/self-reported) · Counter-evidence: – · Confidence: low
- When it applies: every manager order. In Albania: deposit when ordering, balance in cash or bank transfer on delivery, fiscal invoice issued; no open credit for new accounts.

### L-030: B2B buyers already have a supplier; bring a concrete reason to switch and keep them through direct, long relationships
- Evidence: S-039 (corporates buying staff meals were "hooked on" established restaurants), S-034 (sells to Serbian spa hotels mostly on long-standing direct relationships), S-031 (no competitor tried to take its regular customers even after its fire), S-046 (Albanian hotel-textile supplier: long relationships, knows which clients will re-order) (n=4, self-reported) · Counter-evidence: – · Confidence: medium
- When it applies: approaching Tirana managers who already buy from Megateksa or a laundry. Lead with a trial set and a fixed delivery date; once won, the same stickiness protects you.

### L-031: An established local hotel-textile supplier already serves Airbnb apartments; price and pitch against it, not only against Megateksa
- Evidence: S-046 (Gjergji H. Tekstil: since 1994, 25 tailors making custom sizes, Airbnb apartments in Tirana/Durrës/Vlorë buy quality towels and sheets there, sales doubled in 2023, laundry and linen rental planned) (n=1, self-reported interview) · Counter-evidence: – · Confidence: low
- When it applies: pricing and positioning the host kit. Before quoting managers, get the incumbent's prices, sizes,
  minimums and lead times (visit or ask as a buyer). Differentiate on a ready, packed kit per unit, delivery, refills on
  demand and no contract (L-028), since custom sizes and "hotel quality" are what the incumbent already sells. Confirm
  whether its laundry/rental launched (gap 18).

### L-033: Buy from the foreign maker directly, not through local wholesalers
- Evidence: S-052 (Albanian online seller: local suppliers meant high costs and low profit; contracts with foreign suppliers let it sell cheaper), S-050 (Romanian HoReCa firm moved from importing towels to its own brand with European producers; advertorial), S-054 (Kosovo: a wholesaler charged a small shop 88 cents for an item it sold to supermarkets at 75 cents), S-063 (2021 Monitor flower-trade feature: many Albanian florists who first bought from local companies began importing themselves for lower prices and higher margins; market claim in the article), S-071 and S-072 (Tirana used-clothing sellers say big wholesalers raised prices; one 2019 article) (n=5, self-reported) · Counter-evidence: S-003 (a host group-buy plan relied on a factory matching a sample; no counter yet) · Confidence: low
- When it applies: the plan already imports from Turkey; this argues against buying Turkish linen from a Tirana wholesaler to save effort. Keep a local maker only as a fallback (L-003).

### L-034: In Albania, buyers do not prepay strangers; earn deposits with a registered firm, an invoice and a face-to-face sample
- Evidence: S-053 (Albanian marketplace founder: without cash on delivery he would be out of the market; buyers distrust prepayment because informal sellers took money and did not deliver), S-052 (same article: market players say over 90% of online sales are COD), S-010 (Valer Pinderi's NetTrade: over 95% COD in a 2021 Monitor interview, https://monitor.al/e-commerce-pas-nxitjes-ne-pandemi-tani-biznesi-me-i-terhequr/) (n=3, one 2018 article plus one interview; all B2C), S-076 (Movida Albania: anyone can sell from home without registering or guaranteeing goods, helped by cash-only couriers; 2019), S-081 (Graal's manager, 2022: bad experiences with informal sellers make consumers sceptical of buying online), S-083 (Citizens.al, Dec 2024: 70% of surveyed online buyers often got something other than they ordered; Instagram pages refuse returns, some block buyers, and online purchases come without fiscal receipts; buyer evidence), S-087 (Citizens.al, 2022: 80% of 76 respondents bought via Facebook/Instagram and 75% had problems; pages blocked complainers; 18 of 20+ pages had no website, phone or address; buyer evidence) (n=7) · Counter-evidence: – · Confidence: low (no B2B deposit story yet; gap 5)
- When it applies: asking Tirana managers for deposits (L-001, L-029). B2B buyers you meet may accept a small deposit against a fiscal invoice; online strangers will not prepay. What buyers complain about (wrong goods, refused returns, no receipt) is what the kit should visibly promise: the spec in writing, a fiscal receipt with every delivery and a written exchange policy (S-083; our inference).

### L-035: In Tirana and Durrës, single-flat hosts are leaving or handing over daily rentals; sell to operators who are growing, and expect hosts to cut costs
- Evidence: S-057 (host with three flats: 60 euros/night in 2022 fell to 30-40 in 2024 as many copied her; costs included frequent sheet and towel purchases; moved all flats to long-term rent), S-058 (rental-arbitrage host netted 100-400 euros a month and quit), Monitor 2026 market piece (daily rentals concentrating in professional operators and agencies; 15% tax via DIVA for individuals; https://monitor.al/qiraja-afatgjate-fiton-terren-ndersa-ajo-ditore-po-humbet-avantazhin/) (n=2 from one article, anecdote), S-091 (Durrës room renter of 30 years: 2025 income about half of 2024, rates flat 3-4 years), S-092 (Durrës beach district, 2026: nightly price down to 25 euros, cleaning costs rising, most flats now leased to people who manage them) (n=4 from three articles, all self-reported) · Counter-evidence: S-090 (Durrës, 2025: 60-70 euros a night and seven B&B flats in one building, up from a novelty four years earlier), S-059 (2022: a Tirana arbitrage host earned 4x her rent and added a second flat, before the oversupply); listings still grew from 21.3k (Dec 2024) to 27.7k (H1 2026); BIRN reports almost 30,000 units in 2026, +22% · Confidence: medium (run 14: low→medium, three independent articles)
- When it applies: choosing whom to sell to and how to price. Small hosts are price-squeezed and may leave within a season, so they are poor repeat customers; operators that grow (PikHost: ~136 listings per Airbtics, co-founder of the Albanian Vacation Rental Association) are the steadier buyers. Gap 23.

### L-036: Reach new hosts at fit-out, through the people who set flats up
- Evidence: S-059 (a new Tirana host spent 100,000 lekë once on a mattress, kitchen items, washing machine and lights before listing), S-060 (Tirana furnishing firm reports high demand in 2022 for fitting out rented flats for daily rental; budgets go mainly to "stylish" furniture; company claim), S-074 and S-075 (Albanian sanitary-ware importers, Aug 2026: investors' short-term-rental flats now replace families and emigrants as a main source of demand; one article) (n=4 from 3 articles, all company claims) · Counter-evidence: – · Confidence: medium (no story yet of a linen or kit seller reaching hosts at fit-out)
- When it applies: every new listing needs linen once. Furnishers, interior architects and real-estate agents see new hosts at the moment they buy; offer them a ready kit to include or refer (our inference). Gap 24.

### L-037: Hire an accountant before the first sale and settle your VAT status up front
- Evidence: S-065 (Belgrade online shop crossed the VAT threshold without noticing, its accountant quit, a plan to move into a second firm to stay out of VAT proved unworkable, and the owner closed the shop) (n=1, self-reported) · Counter-evidence: – · Confidence: low
- When it applies: from the first import. In Albania an importer will very likely be VAT-registered from the first
  import whatever its turnover (albania-market-context: confirm with DPT), and the 2027 package may cut the threshold
  from ALL 10m to 5m, so price kits with 20% VAT from day one and keep a contracted accountant, not an informal one.

### L-038: Do not run on grants; cover running costs from sales
- Evidence: S-065 (a USAID project paid for staff; when it stopped in early 2025 she paid salaries from savings, laid people off and later closed), S-066 (a 5,000-dollar grant sped up a marketplace that never paid its founders and closed) (n=2, self-reported, both Serbia) · Counter-evidence: S-049 (an Albanian towel maker grew with donor-backed loans for equipment) · Confidence: low
- When it applies: a grant can pay for a one-off asset (equipment, a website); do not hire or sign rent on it.

### L-039: Do not haggle the first order with a new Turkish supplier; negotiate price from the second or third order
- Evidence: S-068 (Serbian curtain firm DaLux, importer of materials from Turkey since the 1990s: every time he cut the price on a first order, the supplier cut the quality) (n=1, self-reported) · Counter-evidence: – · Confidence: low
- When it applies: the 2 sample kits and the first small linen order from Denizli/Bursa makers. Pay the quoted price, fix gsm, size and fibre in writing (L-003), and ask for a discount on the re-order once quality is proven. Confirm with a second Turkish-supplier story (gap 28).

### L-040: In Albania, unregistered sellers will undercut your price; sell to buyers who need an invoice and a supplier that lasts
- Evidence: S-073 (Durrës carpentry: a dozen unregistered workshops nearby pay no taxes and undercut its prices), S-076 (Movida Albania: informal home sellers with cash couriers squeeze formal, tax-paying firms), S-053 (informal sellers took prepayments and did not deliver, so buyers distrust strangers), S-081 (Graal's manager, 2022: of over 600 online pages perhaps 20% are licensed, and informal sellers do not compete on equal terms), S-084 (JYSK Albania: informal shops and markets selling poor goods at the lowest prices hurt it most), S-085 (FPM: about 2,000 of 3,000 Tirana furniture makers informal and undercutting; proper invoicing lets its buyers pay in instalments or with a consumer loan), S-088 (registered clothing shop says unregistered online sellers who skip taxes threaten its survival), S-089 (an unregistered Instagram seller says registration costs would close her business: the cost gap behind informal prices) (n=8, self-reported, seven articles) · Counter-evidence: – · Confidence: medium
- When it applies: pricing the host kit against Instagram linen sellers. Do not race them on price; pitch registered operators, hotels and managers who need a fiscal invoice for their own books, a written guarantee and re-orders next season. Monitor (Aug 2026) reports only 2,016 registered online sellers and a tax-office action on unregistered Instagram/Facebook sellers, so the informal price gap may narrow (our inference; gap 27).

### L-041: Do not enter a niche just because similar sellers nearby seem to do well; copycats crowd the spot and push prices down
- Evidence: S-070 (Tirana used-clothing shop opened next to several similar shops because they seemed to do well; closing after 1.5 years; anecdote), S-057 (Tirana host's nightly price fell from 60 to 30-40 euros as many copied her; anecdote) (n=2, anecdote) · Counter-evidence: – · Confidence: low
- When it applies: before ordering stock, count how many others already sell white hotel linen to Tirana hosts (Gjergji H. Tekstil, Megateksa, Instagram sellers) and what makes the kit different (L-031); if others start copying the kit, compete on service and refills, not price.

### L-042: Price the kit against chain stores' premium home-textile tiers too; Albanian buyers do buy the dearest tier
- Evidence: S-084 (JYSK Albania: the 'gold' tier, its most expensive, sells better than basic and plus; duvets and pillows are its third-best category; six Albanian stores by Oct 2026, moving from franchise to company ownership on 1 Jan 2027) (n=1, large chain, self-reported, 2018) · Counter-evidence: S-077, S-078 (Tirana buyers move to cheaper malls and used clothing) · Confidence: low
- When it applies: before setting kit prices, check JYSK's and Megateksa's prices for white duvets, pillows, sheets and towels, since a host furnishing a flat can buy a 'gold' set from JYSK at a mall. The kit's edge over a chain is delivery to the door as a ready set per unit, refills and a B2B invoice, not the textile alone (our inference; gap 33).

## Other lessons (not directly relevant to the current plan)

### L-016: Rental/subscription models need cheap stock and tight logistics before growth
- Evidence: S-013, S-002, S-027 (a French conciergerie runs linen rental for owners on its own hotel-quality stock; anecdote) (n=3) · Confidence: medium. Relevant only if the plan moves toward linen rental.

### L-023: Don't pick a first product you have never used, in a niche you don't know, on a platform you don't understand
- Evidence: S-021, S-022 (n=2) · Confidence: medium.

### L-032: Do not import lookalikes of a branded product; a legal letter can stop sales overnight
- Evidence: S-047 (teen importer's MP3 player too close to a major brand: had to stop trading, could not ship pending orders) (n=1, self-reported) · Confidence: low.
