# Deep research: the best first business for a beginner in Albania

## The answer in short

**Start with: starter kits for short-term-rental hosts.** These are white Turkish cotton bed sets and towels, plus key lockboxes, sold to Airbnb and Booking hosts and property managers in Tirana and Durres. Do not order any stock until hosts have promised to buy.

**Backup: Petkit pet gadgets (water fountains and feeders).** Do this only if Petkit approves you as a dealer in writing and sends you a real price list.

- **It is the only niche that stays in profit under the hardest test.** The CFO advisor added EUR 450 of running costs over 6 months, assumed only 70% of the stock sells, and raised buy prices and ad costs. Under that test the host kits still made about +EUR 100. Every other niche lost money, from -EUR 260 (pet) to -EUR 550 (espresso).
- **You can count the demand, and it is growing.** There are about 31,800 active short-term-rental listings (+15% a year). Tirana added 948 and Durres 681 new listings in 2025, and every new listing needs 2-3 linen sets. Hosts also replace linen every season, so the same customers buy again.
- **Mistakes are cheap.** White towels and sheets do not expire, break, go out of fashion or need warranty repairs. If they do not sell, you can sell them near cost to guesthouses or cleaning companies.
- **The rules are the simplest of the six niches.** No licence is needed. Towels pay 0% duty from any country. Bed linen pays 0% if the Turkish maker provides an EUR.1 origin certificate (10% without it). The main paperwork is an Albanian label with fibre and care details.
- **You win trust face to face, not with paid ads.** A host can touch a sample, and a property manager buying 10-20 sets is one sale, not 20 risky cash-on-delivery parcels. That suits someone with a day job better than chasing strangers with ads.
- **The honest warning: it does not win on price.** Megateksa sells similar towels and sheets cheaply in its shops. Your kit must be clearly better: white, heavier (550 gsm), hotel-style and delivered as a ready set. If hosts will only pay Megateksa prices, the business makes almost nothing.

## Where the advisors disagreed with the research

Where the advisors disagree, this report takes the more cautious view.

- **"Profit if everything sells" shows what you will earn** → No. None of the plans included the running costs that a VAT-registered importer pays from day one: accountant, invoicing software, packaging and samples, about **EUR 450 over 6 months** (CFO). The advisors' guesses for the accountant alone vary: EUR 50-150 a month (Coach's assumption), EUR 100-500 (one consultancy). Invoicing software costs about 7,000-19,000 ALL a year. The CFO assumed 70% of stock sells and added EUR 450 of costs. On the research's own inputs that gives: hosts **+EUR 411**, pet **-EUR 18**, matcha **-EUR 23**, baby **-EUR 115**, padel **-EUR 262**, espresso **-EUR 328**. With stressed inputs: hosts **about +EUR 100**, pet -260, baby -348, matcha -374, padel -529, espresso -550.
- **"Importers may have to register for VAT – confirm"** → All three advisors treat it as near-certain that importers must register for VAT whatever their turnover (Grant Thornton and PwC summaries). That means monthly VAT returns, accounting costs, and import VAT money locked up until you recover it. Refunds of extra VAT credit are slow, so treat that money as tied up. The general VAT threshold is ALL 10m in most sources, but the tax authority page says ALL 5m.
- **7% cash-on-delivery refusals** → Too optimistic for a new Instagram or TikTok seller. Plan on **12-15%**. Use 7% only for repeat customers or business orders you confirm by phone. Phone every buyer before you send the parcel, and take a deposit on anything over about 15,000 ALL.
- **Host kits: 30% margin (EUR 1,270)** → That needs bed sets at 3,990 ALL, while Megateksa sells a satin-polyester double set for 1,999 ALL and a single cotton set for 2,599 ALL. If you match Megateksa's prices, the margin is **about 15% (EUR 526)**. Under stress plus running costs that turns negative. Plan around the cautious case and make hosts prove the premium price with real orders. The CFO also found that the order (50 sets / 100 bath / 100 hand towels) is used up exactly by 25 kits, so nothing is left for top-up sales. His corrected all-sold figure is **EUR 1,229**.
- **Host kits: EUR 6 courier per kit** → A kit weighs about 5-7 kg, so plan **EUR 8-12** outside Tirana (Marketer). The real sales cost is your own evening and weekend time, not EUR 15.
- **Host kits are B2B, so there is no 14-day return right** → Most hosts are private individuals declaring income through DIVA, so legally these are consumer sales. They have the 14-day withdrawal right and 2-year liability. Seal every item and write a clear returns policy.
- **Megateksa caps towel prices** → Partly true. Its cheap 500 gsm towels (859/459 ALL) are beige or green. White 500 gsm comes only in 30x50 (169 ALL), and a 450 gsm 70x140 costs 1,259 ALL. There is more room on price for **white** hotel towels (Coach).
- **Lockboxes earn 51%** → Only inside a kit. Sold alone with courier and ad costs they earn about **22%** (CFO). Temu sells them duty-free.
- **Pet: freight EUR 200** → Petkit ships from China and adds a 10% delivery charge on orders under US$1,000. Plan **EUR 400** freight. The margin then falls from about EUR 618 to **about EUR 400 (about 14%)** (CFO).
- **Pet: fountain at 1.3-1.5x EU retail makes 16%** → The fountain costs EUR 66-75 in EU shops, so 9,900 ALL is 1.5-1.65x. At an ad cost of EUR 18-25 per sale the fountain makes almost nothing. The feeder and filters are where the profit is.
- **Pet: buy one demo litter box** → That is EUR 437, about 19% of the budget, in one unit that becomes hard to sell. Skip it and take paid deposits only.
- **Matcha: highest Trends score (33.2) means real demand for kits** → "matcha" is a general word, and the rising searches ("matcha near me", "how to make matcha latte") are about cafes and drinking. They do not show demand for 7,500 ALL kits (Marketer).
- **Matcha: Kusmi 30 g costs 3,499 ALL** → It now shows at **2,999 ALL**. Assume about 3,000 ALL is the street price for a 30 g tin.
- **Matcha: profit about EUR 609** → The EUR 12 buy price predates the 2026 tencha price jump (1.7-2.3x). The figure also leaves out the AKU licence (premises and a technical director). Realistic: **EUR 0-100 before licence costs, negative after** (CFO). A Christmas launch is impossible from 23 Nov 2026; Valentine's 2027 is the earliest.
- **Matcha: a single tin loses money** → For a first sale bought with ads, yes. A repeat buyer reordering by direct message earns about +14%, so matcha's strength is repeat customers (Marketer). The licence problem still decides the matter.
- **Padel: prices "about 10% above EU street"** → They are really +11% to +24%. At true EU street prices the order only breaks even (**EUR 2**), and under stress it loses **-EUR 113** (CFO). Clubs would also want a 15-30% commission.
- **Padel: "padel tirana" up 80%** → Most of that came from the FIP event in May 2026 (about 20,000 spectators). Spectators do not buy rackets.
- **Espresso: priced "15-20% above EU retail" makes EUR 167** → The Gaggia price in that plan is **33%** above EU retail. At a believable +15% the order loses about **EUR 150-250** (CFO).
- **Baby: Ergobaby buy price at 45-48% of the shop price** → That is a top-tier dealer price. A beginner would more likely pay 10-25% more, which makes the Embrace and Evolve earn about 1%. The baby niche's regulation score should also be lower (about 3-4), because the importer carries infant-safety liability.

### First order, with numbers

The research plan is a full order: 50 bed sets, 100 bath towels, 100 hand towels and 30 lockboxes, **EUR 2,217 in cash**, about EUR 1,270 if everything sells at premium prices. The advisors cut it to a pre-sold half-size order. **Size the order to the number of hosts who commit:**

- **20 or more kits committed:** use the full research order (EUR 2,217).
- **10-19 kits committed:** use the half-size order below.
- **Under 10 committed:** do not order. Under 5 at 17,500 ALL: change the price or stop.

**Half-size order (my recalculation from the research inputs).** 28 bed sets, 60 bath towels and 60 hand towels from one Turkish maker with an EUR.1, sent by road groupage. That is enough for 12 kits plus a few top-ups. Optionally add 15 lockboxes by air.

- Goods EUR 850. Freight EUR 180-300 (no quote yet, and freight may not halve with a smaller order). Fixed costs EUR 182: broker 100, scanning fee 22, Turkish export papers and EUR.1 about 60. Labels about EUR 15. Import VAT EUR 206-230, which you get back later. **Cash at import: about EUR 1,430-1,580.**
- 15 lockboxes: goods 66, air 60, duty 15%, clearance 45, so about **EUR 12.76 landed each** (not 8.96, because the fixed costs are spread over fewer boxes). About EUR 220 in cash including VAT.
- Add samples (EUR 100-150): **total about EUR 1,750-1,950.** If your budget is only EUR 1,500, ask committed hosts for a deposit (for example 30% of the kit price) to cover the gap.
- **Contribution per kit at 17,530 ALL:** about **EUR 26-34 (16-21%)**, assuming EUR 10 courier, 7% refusals (phone-confirmed), EUR 15 to win the sale and a 3% returns reserve. **At Megateksa-matched prices (about 14,200 ALL): about -EUR 3.5 to +EUR 5 per kit.**
- **If 12 kits and the top-ups all sell at premium prices:** about **EUR 360-480 contribution (16-21%)**. After the 6-month running costs (EUR 450) that is **about -EUR 90 to +EUR 30**.

So the first half-size batch is mainly a paid lesson in how importing works. The money comes from repeat orders and the second, larger order, because the fixed costs (broker, freight minimum, accountant) do not shrink with a smaller order. This is the more cautious view, and it is why the pre-commitments matter.

### Paperwork

1. Register a business at QKB. Plan on **VAT registration from your first import**, with monthly returns. Confirm with a Tirana accountant first.
2. Buy fiscal invoicing software (about 7,000-19,000 ALL a year). Every sale needs a real-time fiscal invoice, including cash-on-delivery sales.
3. **EUR.1 or invoice origin declaration** from the Turkish **maker**. Without it, bed linen pays 10% duty. Towels are 0% anyway. Goods that an Istanbul wholesaler resells from Pakistan, India or China are not Turkish origin.
4. Customs: broker about EUR 80-150, scanning fee EUR 22 if the declaration is over EUR 1,000 (EUR 5 below), 20% import VAT that you recover later.
5. An Albanian label on every unit (stickers are fine): fibre content, size, care instructions, importer name and address. Laws 10489/2011 and 10480/2011 cover product safety. Follow the EU labelling format to be safe.
6. Consumer law: a 14-day withdrawal right and 2-year liability for private hosts. Seal each item. Companies registered for VAT buy on business terms.
7. Profit tax is 0% up to ALL 14m turnover until 2029. A 2027 tax package (VAT 21%, threshold ALL 5m) is pending but not yet law.

### Risks and how to reduce them

| Risk | How to reduce it |
|---|---|
| Hosts will only pay Megateksa prices | Pre-sell with samples at 17,500 ALL a kit; stop if fewer than 5 agree. Compete on white, heavier, hotel-grade goods and convenience. |
| The supplier is a reseller, so there is no EUR.1 | Get it in writing that they make the goods and will issue an EUR.1. If not, bed linen pays 10% duty (kit margin falls to about 15%). |
| Quality complaints (thin towels, shrinking, colour fading) | Weigh samples to check the gsm and wash them 3 times before ordering. Compare side by side with Megateksa. |
| Bad timing (hosts buy in February-May) | Order in January-February. Stock that arrives in June sits until autumn. |
| Minimum orders of 250-500 pieces | Use DENIB to find small makers. Accept a slightly higher unit price, but only with an EUR.1. |
| Selling takes time you do not have because of your day job | Target property managers and cleaning companies (one buyer, many sets) and host Facebook groups. |
| Lockboxes are cheaper on Temu | Sell them only inside the kit. If the Temu check is bad, leave them out of the first order. |
| Cash-on-delivery refusals | Phone-confirm every order, take deposits, and ask business buyers to pay by bank transfer. |
| Import VAT money locked up | Keep at least a third of the budget in reserve (Marketer). |

## Your 90-day plan

Today is 23 Nov 2026. The goal is stock in hand by late February for the March-May season.

**Week 1 (23-29 Nov)**
- Email Beyaz Nevresim and DENIB, and contact Hursan. Ask for prices ex-works for 50-100 pieces per item in white, the minimum order, whether they make the goods, whether they issue an EUR.1, and sample prices.
- Book a meeting with a Tirana accountant. Confirm that importers must register for VAT, the monthly fee and which invoicing software to use.
- Join the "Airbnb Hosts in Albania" Facebook group and other host groups. Start a list of 30 hosts, property managers and cleaning companies in Tirana and Durres.

**Week 2 (30 Nov-6 Dec)**
- Order samples from 2-3 suppliers, plus 2-3 lockbox samples (budget **EUR 100-150**, maximum **EUR 200** before the first order).
- Ask an Albanian forwarder (for example Dyrrahsped) for a written Turkey-Tirana groupage quote for about 0.5 m3 and 1 m3, plus the broker fee.
- Visit Megateksa and Zara Home in person. Feel their towels and note the real prices of white hotel lines.
- At no cost, email Petkit (sales@petkit.com) about dealer approval for Albania, as the backup.

**Week 3 (7-13 Dec)**
- Check Temu prices for lockboxes.
- When samples arrive: weigh them for gsm, wash them 3 times, check shrinkage and colour, and compare them with Megateksa.
- Make 2 sample kits and set prices (kit 17,530 ALL, bed set 3,990, bath towel 1,090, hand towel 550).

**Week 4 (14-20 Dec)**
- Show the samples to 15-20 hosts and property managers. Ask each one: how many kits, at what price, for what date, whether they will pay a deposit, and whether they are VAT-registered or pay by bank transfer.
- **Stop or change course if** any of these happen:
  - No supplier quotes within about 15% of EUR 16 / 4.60 / 2.10 together with an EUR.1.
  - The freight quote is above EUR 300.
  - Samples fail the wash test.

**Month 2 (January 2027)**
- Count your commitments and size the order as described above (20+ full, 10-19 half, under 10 none).
- Register the business and VAT, set up the invoicing software and a business bank account, and print the Albanian labels.
- Collect deposits. Place the Turkish order by mid-January with an EUR.1 on the paperwork. Order lockboxes only if the Temu check and the kit commitments support it.
- **Stop if** fewer than 10 kits are committed, or hosts only accept prices near Megateksa's (a kit at about 14,200 ALL leaves roughly zero).

**Month 3 (February 2027)**
- Clear customs with the broker (EUR.1 checked), put on the labels, deliver the committed kits in person or by courier, and issue a fiscal invoice for every sale.
- Track for each kit: real landed cost, delivery cost, refusals, returns and hours spent. Ask each host for referrals and a reorder date.
- **Decision rules:**
  - Reorder only if the real contribution per kit is at least **EUR 25** and at least 70% of the committed kits have been paid.
  - If refusals go above **15%**, switch to deposit or bank-transfer only.
  - If less than **50%** of the stock is sold by the end of May, do not reorder. Sell the rest at cost to guesthouses or cleaners.
  - Keep at least a third of the budget unspent until the first batch has paid back.

## Niches to avoid for now and why

- **Matcha (as a food import).** It has the strongest social-media appeal and the only built-in reorders, but:
  - The AKU food licence may need premises and a technical director.
  - Duty is 15% on Japanese tea and ceramics, and the order needs 2 shipments and food border checks.
  - Matchai, OhMyCha, Bloom and Kusmi already sell at about 3,000 ALL per 30 g. Temu sells tea-ware kits under the EUR 22 duty-free limit.
  - At most, run a 2-week content test with pre-orders, or ask Matchai or OhMyCha about selling tea-ware next to their matcha. Import no food until AKU answers in writing.
- **Premium baby gear.** The "missing brands" idea was wrong: Bugaboo, Stokke, Cybex, BabyBjorn, Joolz, Doona and Medela are all sold locally at 10-25% above EU prices. Births are falling (about 21-22.5k a year). Parents trust shops with showrooms. Ergobaby only works with a dealer account, and imports bought at EU retail lose 8-24%.
- **Padel.** The player base is tiny and centred on Tirana. smash.al already sells at EU prices. Rackets pay 15% duty and lose 40-50% of their value each season. Clubs control the customers and would want a commission.
- **Espresso gear.** There is almost no demand ("gaggia" 0.1). EU shops already sell to Albanians in lek. Machines bring 2-year warranty risk with no service network. The plan loses money at believable prices, and the accessories do not sell without machines.
